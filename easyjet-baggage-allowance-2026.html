Budget airline rules: easyJet is a low-cost carrier — the headline fare only buys you a seat and one small under-seat bag. Everything else (large cabin bag, hold luggage, seat selection, speedy boarding) costs extra. Buy online in advance to get the lowest prices — airport and gate fees are much higher.

Personal items allowed

In addition to your small cabin bag, you may also bring these items on board free of charge:

  • Coat, jacket, or blanket
  • Umbrella or walking stick
  • Small bag of duty-free purchases (from the airport)
  • Reading material
  • Infant food for consumption on board
  • Mobility aids (wheelchairs, crutches, etc.)

Gate fee warning: If you arrive at the gate with a bag that is too big for the under-seat sizer and you have not paid for a large cabin bag, easyJet will put it in the hold and charge you £45 or more at the gate. This is significantly more expensive than buying online in advance. Always measure your bag before you fly.

Price increases over time: easyJet hold luggage prices go up as the flight fills up and as you get closer to the departure date. The cheapest prices are available when you first book. Adding bags later through Manage Bookings costs more, and buying at the airport is the most expensive option.

Check-In Policy

easyJet check-in is done online before you arrive at the airport. There is no traditional airport check-in desk service.

Online Check-In Timeline

FLEXI fare & easyJet Plus members — Check-in opens 30 days before departure. You can select your seat and download your boarding pass early.
60 days before
With paid seat selection — If you pay to choose your seat on a Standard fare, check-in opens 60 days before departure.
24 hours before
Standard fare (no seat selection) — Check-in opens 24 hours before departure. Seats are assigned randomly.
2 hours before
Check-in closes — Online check-in closes 2 hours before your flight departs. You must have your boarding pass before arriving at the airport.

How to check in

  • Online: Via easyjet.com or the easyJet mobile app
  • What you need: Booking reference and surname
  • Boarding pass: Download to your phone (mobile boarding pass) or print it
  • Bag drop: If you have hold luggage, go to the easyJet bag drop counter with your boarding pass
  • Cost: Online check-in is free for all passengers

Important: You must check in online before arriving at the airport. easyJet does not operate traditional check-in desks — you will find bag drop counters and customer service desks, but you need your boarding pass already issued. If you arrive without checking in, you may be charged a fee or miss your flight.

Delayed, Lost & Damaged Baggage

If your baggage is delayed, lost, or damaged on an easyJet flight, you have rights under the Montreal Convention.

Your Rights Under the Montreal Convention

easyJet is liable for delayed, lost, or damaged checked baggage. The maximum liability is approximately 1,288 SDR (~£1,600 / €1,885 / $2,000) per passenger.

Delayed baggage

  • Report immediately: Go to the easyJet baggage service desk or lost property office at the airport before leaving
  • Get a PIR: Property Irregularity Report — keep this reference number
  • Essential items: You can claim reimbursement for essential purchases (toiletries, basic clothing) while your bag is delayed
  • Keep receipts: Keep all receipts for purchases you make due to the delay
  • Claim deadline: Submit your claim within 21 days of receiving your baggage
  • Track online: Use the WorldTracer system with your PIR reference

Lost baggage

  • After 21 days: Baggage is considered lost if not found within 21 days
  • Compensation: You can claim the value of your lost belongings, up to the Montreal Convention limit
  • Proof required: You may need to provide proof of value (receipts, photos, etc.)
  • Bag fee refund: easyJet should refund your hold luggage fee if your bag is lost

Damaged baggage

  • Report immediately: Report damage at the airport before leaving
  • Take photos: Photograph the damage and your baggage tag
  • Claim deadline: Submit your claim within 7 days of receiving your baggage
  • Repair or replace: easyJet may offer to repair the bag or compensate you
  • Exclusions: Normal wear and tear, overpacking damage, and fragile items may not be covered

How to file a claim: You can file a baggage claim through easyJet's website or customer service. You will need your PIR reference number, booking reference, and receipts for any expenses. Claims are typically processed within a few weeks. If easyJet rejects your claim, you can escalate to Aviation ADR or the relevant national aviation authority.

Fare Types & Baggage Overview

easyJet offers three main fare types. The baggage allowance increases significantly with higher fares.

Standard

  • 1 small cabin bag (45 × 36 × 20 cm)
  • Large cabin bag — extra cost
  • Hold luggage — extra cost
  • Seat selection — extra cost
  • Speedy boarding — extra cost
  • Flight changes — fee applies

Standard Plus

  • 1 small cabin bag
  • 1 large cabin bag (56 × 45 × 25 cm)
  • Hold luggage — extra cost
  • Seat selection — extra cost
  • Speedy boarding — extra cost
  • Flight changes — fee applies

FLEXI / Inclusive Plus

  • 1 small cabin bag
  • 1 large cabin bag
  • 1 × 23 kg hold bag
  • Up Front seat selection
  • Speedy Boarding
  • Free flight changes

easyJet Plus Membership

easyJet Plus is an annual membership that adds benefits to every flight you book:

  • Cost: £249 per year
  • Large cabin bag on every flight
  • Speedy Boarding on every flight
  • Dedicated bag drop counter
  • Fast-track security at selected airports
  • Early check-in (30 days before)
  • Extra legroom seat selection

Is FLEXI worth it? If you need a large cabin bag + a 23 kg hold bag + seat selection, compare the cost of adding these individually to a Standard fare versus the FLEXI fare upgrade. On many routes, FLEXI works out cheaper than buying everything separately — plus you get free flight changes and Speedy Boarding.

Infant & Child Baggage

easyJet offers baggage options for infants and children.

Infants (under 2 years, on lap)

  • Infant fare: Flat fee per flight (varies by route)
  • Carry-on: 1 additional cabin bag for baby items (max 15 kg)
  • Free checked items: 1 foldable pushchair / stroller + 1 car seat
  • Hold luggage: No free hold baggage — must be purchased separately
  • Baby food: Allowed in cabin in reasonable quantities

Children (2+ years, own seat)

  • Full adult baggage allowance: Same as adults in the same fare type
  • Small cabin bag: Free (45 × 36 × 20 cm)
  • Large cabin bag: Paid extra or included in higher fares
  • Hold luggage: Paid extra or included in FLEXI
  • Free stroller / car seat: Still allowed until age 2

Stroller policy: One fully foldable pushchair or stroller per infant/child is carried free of charge, in addition to your baggage allowance. You can drop it off at the gate — it will be waiting for you at the baggage hall on arrival. Car seats are also free.

Sports Equipment & Musical Instruments

easyJet accepts sports equipment and musical instruments for an additional fee.

Sports equipment

  • Types accepted: Skis/snowboards, golf bags, bicycles, diving equipment, etc.
  • Fee: From ~£30 online per item (varies by route and equipment type)
  • Weight limit: Counts toward your hold luggage weight; standard hold baggage rules apply
  • Must be booked in advance: Add during booking or via Manage Bookings
  • Proper packing required: Must be in a suitable protective case or bag
  • Bicycles: Must be properly packed / boxed; pedal and handlebar removal may be required

Musical instruments

  • Small instruments: Can be carried as cabin baggage if within small cabin bag limits (45 × 36 × 20 cm)
  • Medium instruments: Can travel in the cabin if you buy an extra seat (must fit in the seat)
  • Large instruments: Must be checked in as hold baggage
  • Fragile items: easyJet has limited liability for damage to fragile items
  • Hard case recommended: Always use a hard protective case

Book sports equipment early: Sports equipment fees on easyJet increase as the flight fills up, just like regular hold luggage. Add your sports equipment when you book to get the lowest price. Some equipment types may have limited availability on each flight, so book early to secure space.

Other Special Items

  • Wheelchairs & mobility aids: Carried free of charge, in addition to your baggage allowance
  • Medical equipment & medication: Allowed in cabin with proper documentation; may count toward your allowance
  • Duty-free purchases: Must fit within your cabin bag allowance if bought before security
  • Assistive devices: Priority handling for passengers with reduced mobility
  • Fragile items: Must be properly packed; limited liability
  • Wedding dress: Can be carried on board if it fits within cabin bag limits, or checked in as hold luggage

Frequently Asked Questions

What is easyJet's carry-on baggage allowance? ▼

Every easyJet passenger gets one free small cabin bag that fits under the seat:

  • Small cabin bag (free): 45 × 36 × 20 cm, up to 15 kg — included with every fare
  • Large cabin bag (paid): 56 × 45 × 25 cm, up to 15 kg — costs extra

The large cabin bag costs from about £6–£25 when bought online in advance, but jumps to £45 or more if you get caught at the gate with an oversized bag.

Large cabin bags are included with:

  • FLEXI / Inclusive Plus fares
  • easyJet Plus annual membership
  • Up Front and Extra Legroom seat purchases (on some routes)

Always measure your bag before flying — easyJet uses sizer boxes at the gate and is strict about enforcement.

How much does easyJet hold luggage cost? ▼

easyJet hold luggage (checked baggage) is a paid add-on on all fares. Prices vary significantly based on route, season, demand, and how early you book.

Online prices (when added during booking):

  • 15 kg hold bag: from ~£6.99
  • 23 kg hold bag: from ~£9.49
  • Extra weight top-ups: in 3 kg increments up to 32 kg

Airport prices:

  • 23 kg hold bag at the airport: £48
  • Gate-checked bag: £45+

Key points:

  • Maximum 3 hold bags per passenger
  • Maximum 32 kg per bag (hard limit)
  • Maximum size: 275 cm total (length + width + height)
  • Prices go up as the flight fills up — book early
  • Buying online is always cheaper than at the airport
What happens if easyJet delays my baggage? ▼

If easyJet delays your checked baggage, you have rights under the Montreal Convention:

What to do immediately:

  • Report the delay at the airport baggage service desk before leaving
  • Get a PIR (Property Irregularity Report) with a reference number
  • Take photos of your baggage tag and any damage
  • Keep all receipts for essential purchases

Compensation:

  • Maximum liability: ~1,288 SDR (~£1,600 / €1,885)
  • Reimbursement for essential items (toiletries, clothing) while delayed
  • Full value of lost items if bag is never found

Deadlines:

  • Delayed baggage: claim within 21 days of receiving your bag
  • Damaged baggage: claim within 7 days
  • Lost baggage: after 21 days, bag is considered lost

File your claim through easyJet's website or customer service. If your claim is rejected, you can escalate to Aviation ADR or the CAA.

Check-in closes 2 hours before departure.

Does easyJet weigh carry-on bags? ▼

easyJet's carry-on policy is primarily size-based, not weight-based. The official weight limit is 15 kg, but in practice, easyJet is much more focused on bag size than weight.

What easyJet does check:

  • Size: Very strict about bag dimensions. Sizer boxes are used at the gate to check if bags fit.
  • Number of bags: Only one small cabin bag unless you paid for a large one.

What easyJet rarely checks:

  • Weight: They do not typically weigh carry-on bags at the gate. The 15 kg limit is more of a safety/handling guideline than a strictly enforced rule.

However, if your bag is clearly very heavy and the gate agent notices, they may ask you to check it. And if it does not fit in the sizer box, you will definitely be charged the gate fee (£45+).

Bottom line: Focus on getting the size right. Weight is less of a concern with easyJet compared to airlines like JAL or Ryanair that strictly weigh carry-on bags.

Can I take a handbag as well as hand luggage on easyJet? ▼

On easyJet, the small cabin bag (45 × 36 × 20 cm) is your one free carry-on item. This includes handbags, backpacks, laptop bags, and any other personal item — they all count as your one small cabin bag.

What counts as your one free bag:

  • Handbag / purse
  • Backpack
  • Laptop bag
  • Any other bag that fits under the seat

You cannot bring both a handbag AND a backpack on a Standard fare — they would count as two bags and you would need to pay for a large cabin bag or check one in.

Additional items that are free (don't count as your bag):

  • Coat or jacket
  • Umbrella
  • Walking stick / crutches
  • Duty-free purchases (from airport shops after security)
  • Reading material
  • Infant food

Tip: If you need to bring more, pay for a large cabin bag in advance online — it is much cheaper than the gate fee.

What is easyJet Plus and is it worth it? ▼

easyJet Plus is an annual membership program that costs £249 per year. It adds benefits to every easyJet flight you book.

What's included:

  • Large cabin bag (56 × 45 × 25 cm) on every flight
  • Speedy Boarding on every flight
  • Fast-track security at selected airports
  • Dedicated bag drop counter
  • Early check-in (30 days before)
  • Extra Legroom / Up Front seat selection

Is it worth it?

  • If you fly easyJet 10+ times per year and would otherwise pay for a large cabin bag each time, yes — it pays for itself quickly.
  • If you only fly easyJet a few times a year, it is probably cheaper to just pay for the large cabin bag per flight.
  • Consider how much you value Speedy Boarding, fast-track security, and seat selection — these perks add up for frequent flyers.

Compare: a large cabin bag costs ~£6–£25 per flight. At £249/year, you break even at about 10–15 flights per year, depending on the route.
