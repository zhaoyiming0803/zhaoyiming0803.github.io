EU 100cm 7kg Carry-on Rule Explained

What does "100 cm total size" actually mean? Will your current suitcase fit? Here is everything you need to know about the new EU cabin bag size limit.

Original Content: EU Free Cabin Bag Rules 2027 – New Carry-on Law Explained

What Is the 100cm Rule?

Under the new EU free cabin bag regulation taking effect in 2027, the free wheeled cabin bag must meet a maximum total linear dimension of approximately 100 cm (length + width + height), with a maximum weight of 7 kg.

Linear dimension = length + width + height
Instead of setting specific measurements for each side, the EU rule limits the total sum of all three dimensions. This gives airlines and passengers more flexibility in bag shape.

How to Measure Your Bag

Measuring linear dimensions is simple:

  1. Measure the length (longest side) of your bag
  2. Measure the width (second longest side)
  3. Measure the height (shortest side)
  4. Add all three numbers together

If the total is ≤ 100 cm and the weight is ≤ 7 kg, your bag qualifies as a free cabin bag under the EU rule.

Important: Always include wheels, handles, and any external pockets in your measurement. Airlines measure at the largest points — not just the main compartment.

Wait — 100cm Seems Small

If you are used to the standard 55×40×20 cm (115 cm total) carry-on size, 100 cm might sound tight. That is because it is smaller than what many full-service airlines allow today.

But remember: the EU rule sets a minimum standard. Airlines can choose to allow larger bags for free — they just cannot charge extra for a bag that meets the 100 cm / 7 kg minimum.

Key point: The 100 cm / 7 kg rule is the minimum that must be included for free. Airlines can still allow larger bags. Full-service carriers like Lufthansa or Air France will probably keep their current 55×40×20 cm limits, which are more generous than the EU minimum.

Personal Item: 40 × 30 × 15 cm

In addition to the wheeled cabin bag, you also get a free personal item that must fit under the seat:

  • Maximum size: 40 × 30 × 15 cm
  • Must fit: Under the seat in front of you
  • Examples: Backpack, laptop bag, purse, small tote

This is slightly different from the personal item size on some airlines today. For example, Ryanair's current under-seat limit is 40×20×25 cm (85 cm total), which is flatter but wider.

What About Soft Bags?

Soft-sided bags (backpacks, duffels) can be tricky because they can expand or compress. The general rule:

  • Airlines measure bags as they are presented at the gate
  • If your bag is overstuffed and bulging, it counts as larger
  • Soft bags that can be squeezed into a sizer box usually pass
  • Hard-shell bags are measured at their maximum dimensions (including wheels and handles)

Tips for Traveling Light

🎒 Use a backpack

Backpacks are flexible and often fit under seats even when they technically exceed the dimensions on paper.

⚖️ Weigh your bag

7 kg is about 15.4 lbs. Invest in a small luggage scale to avoid surprises at the gate.

👕 Pack light

Roll clothes instead of folding. Use packing cubes. Wear bulkiest items on the plane.

📐 Measure everything

Don't forget wheels and handles. They add 5–10 cm to the total size of most rolling suitcases.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 100cm the exact official number? ▼

The EU regulation sets a framework for free cabin bags, but the exact final dimensions will be confirmed in the implementing acts. The 100 cm / 7 kg figure is based on the proposed text and media reports. Check the official EU sources for the final exact numbers.

Will my 20-inch carry-on still work? ▼

A standard 20-inch carry-on (about 55×40×20 cm = 115 cm total) is larger than the 100 cm EU minimum. However, many airlines may continue to allow larger bags — the EU rule only sets a minimum, not a maximum. Check your specific airline's policy.

Do wheels and handles count? ▼

Yes. Airlines always measure at the widest/tallest/longest points, which includes wheels, handles, and external pockets. A bag that is "55 cm body only" might be 60+ cm with wheels and handles extended.

What if my bag is slightly over? ▼

It depends on the airline and the airport. Some are strict and use sizer boxes; others are more lenient. But to be safe, your bag should fit within the stated limits. Gate fees for oversized bags can be €50–100 or more.

Can I bring both a cabin bag and a personal item? ▼

Yes — under the new EU rule, the base fare includes both a wheeled cabin bag (overhead bin) and a personal item (under seat). That is two items total, both for free.
